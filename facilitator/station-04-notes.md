
## Organizer note — keep out of the starting card

Version 1 results figure and note are generated from the same dataset as Stations 1 and 2.
Supply a verified prepared result rather than waiting for Station 2's live output.
The numerical pattern must come from the same synthetic study used at the other
stations. Do not pick a separate result to make a preferred purchasing decision.

The results note states group sizes, events, follow-up, what each curve
represents, and which analyses were actually performed. It should supply enough
information to interpret the figure without making participants guess missing
study facts. Do not include a ready-made purchasing conclusion.

This station translates the trial results into a recommendation. Station 3 writes
the external scientific background; Station 4 need not independently review its
papers. No real communication or purchase takes place. Keep that disclosure, but clarify
the role: recommendations should address the study's evidence within the story,
not dismiss the exercise because the data are simulated. Supply factual results
and definitions, not the intended purchasing argument. A factual note can prime
an answer too if it already explains why the machine is or is not justified.

## Check slip · release at 14:00

Underline the factual claim carrying the recommendation. Find its support in the
results note or figure: the group comparison, time point and uncertainty, if
reported. Does the text preserve the endpoint—manuscript acceptance—and the
comparison actually studied? What extra assumption connects that finding to
the purchase? Keep or revise the reply and explain why.

A visual gap alone does not establish statistical significance. An unreported
median is not available merely because the agent supplies one. A careful or
conditional recommendation is valid, but not mandatory if the reasoning supports
another choice. No specific headline or conclusion is the answer key.

## Reflection · release at 14:10

Use `delegation-reflection.md`. If needed, notice who chose the recommendation,
which findings to emphasize, the tone, or the caveats. Ask: “Who decided what
would justify the purchase?” Ask what the team accepted
knowingly and what they noticed only on review. Accepting the agent's whole draft
can be a considered choice.

If the agent stalls, participants draft two sentences from the prepared result
and describe the follow-up they would have requested. Record that the live-agent
experience was incomplete rather than presenting this as equivalent.
