# What did we hand over?

Facilitator design note · applies to all four stations · 17 September 2026.
Not part of the initial participant brief or the files uploaded to an agent.

## Learning to carry into everyday work

Delegation includes decisions as well as work. It is possible to hand over nearly
all operational choices. The point is to notice what was delegated and decide
whether that is the amount and kind of delegation you want for this task.
More delegation is not a lower score, and intervening more is not automatically
better. Asking the agent to choose can be deliberate and useful.

## First let people work

Give each station a concrete situation, its materials and a useful deliverable.
Do not ask teams to choose an “open” or “controlled” prompting style, announce a
quiz about delegation, or require them to specify every decision first.
Do not withhold information needed to do the work, introduce traps, or treat
participants' convenience as a fault. Method hints are available when needed.

All stations follow the same sequence:

- 13:40–14:00: produce an output with the agent and improve it as desired.
- 14:00–14:10: check one important part against data or an original source.
- 14:10–14:15: two-minute reflection, then choose the report; use remaining time as buffer.

This replaces part of the previous report-preparation buffer, not extra session time.

## Reveal only after the output and check

Ask teams to look at their actual output and conversation:

1. **What decisions are built into this result?** Pick two examples.
2. **Who made them?** Us, the agent, or both? Did we notice at the time?
3. **Would we delegate them the same way next time? Why?**

A decision can be proposed by the agent and later consciously accepted by the
team. Retaining a good decision is a valid choice. Include facilitator suggestions
rather than attributing those to the team or agent.

If a team says “everything” or “nothing”, ask for one concrete example. Do not
score or count decisions as a competition. Do not require correction merely to
show that the participants did something.

## Prompts for facilitators, only if the reflection needs support

| Station | Decisions to notice in the finished output |
|---|---|
| 1: Data | Which records to retain, how to interpret missing dates, what counts as an acceptance or observation end |
| 2: Figure | Analysis and display, axis direction, reference group, uncertainty, headline |
| 3: Literature | Which claim to emphasize, whether studies answer our actual question, strength of language |
| 4: Recommendation | Which result to foreground, caveats to include, tone and purchasing recommendation |

The four coffee stations follow this structure; version 1 datasets and prepared
outputs are available in the material packs. Preserve genuine choice in
methods/presentation while supplying clear factual definitions in the study data.

## Station report and final transfer

Within each station's existing two-minute report, include one example:

**“The agent decided ___. We noticed when ___. Next time we would ___.”**

At 14:25–14:30, each participant completes the personal transfer already planned:

**“For ___ in my own work, I would let the agent decide ___. Before using the
result, I would want to understand or decide ___ myself.”**

Closing thought: **“The agent can do the work and make many of the choices.
What matters is recognizing which choices you are comfortable handing over.”**

The useful result and independent check remain part of the session. This reflection
connects them to conscious delegation; it does not replace either one.
